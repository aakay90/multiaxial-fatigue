# Multiaxial fatigue methods: preliminary implementation feasibility

Assessment date: 8 October 2026. Repository: andreachiocca/multiaxial-fatigue, GitHub main at 01418383ac2ab3cea8bab225f718dd652ba82820; inspected local tree f7beb7b92be42459d9237df7a50e4bd2369a5a7e. Source review: Review-FABER-WG43.md, supplied in this Work thread. No application code, tests, data, repository documentation or Git refs were changed for this assessment.

## Decision in brief

The application is a good platform for additional local, predominantly elastic, constant-amplitude multiaxial criteria. The strongest first additions are Crossland and Sines as invariant benchmarks; Papadopoulos and MWCM as established complementary approaches; and Wang 2022, Zhang–Yao, Qi, Gonçalves and Matsubara as relatively self-contained research criteria. Faithful restoration of Carpinteri–Spagnoli, Dang Van and deviatoric SWT deserves its own workstream. Their archived files are not usable implementations.

The shortlist below deliberately includes more than the easiest methods. It includes specialized and data-demanding A/B candidates, with lower priorities and explicit boundaries. A high implementation priority does not assert superior predictive accuracy. Conversely, unavailable material data or equations can delay a technically feasible method without making it category D.

The review is not internally consistent. Its 51 numbered rows are not 51 distinct new criteria, and it has additional detailed sections outside that table. Particularly important corrections concern the duplicated Anes entry, Tao 2021, Mérot 2024 and Nguyen 2025. These are reconciled before the shortlist. The report includes a row for every numbered entry, plus the additional detailed sections and relevant comparison methods.

## 1. Evidence and classification rules

**R** means directly inspected repository behavior or data. **V** means stated in the supplied review; it remains secondary evidence, not an independent verification of the original formulation. **P** means a targeted primary publisher/source check, listed at the end. **J** means my engineering feasibility judgment. A/B/C/D and complexity/priority are J assessments based on R and V/P.

- **A — directly implementable:** chiefly a new scalar criterion or local criterion evaluator within the existing harmonic, constant-amplitude workflow. Ordinary numerical operations inside that evaluator do not by themselves make a method B.
- **B — limited extensions:** additional material fields, a different plane-selection procedure, prescribed cyclic strain input, a material-specific calibration, a specialized life solver, or a bounded numerical helper. Data may still need to be supplied.
- **C — major extensions:** a general variable-amplitude history/counting/damage architecture, constitutive integration, spatial FE/geometry processing, crack-growth simulation, or anisotropic/multiscale material modeling.
- **D — not an identifiable implementation proposal:** an unresolved attribution, unspecified variant, or study that does not supply an independent criterion. D is not used merely because complete equations are missing.

**Scope matters.** A faithful constant-amplitude special case of a wider method may be B while the full variable-amplitude implementation is C. Every such restriction is explicit. This does not license replacing a published path-dependent formulation with a phase-angle proxy, dropping plastic work, substituting a different amplitude definition, or imposing the repository's generic Basquin fit on a method whose original life law differs.

**Notation in tables:** S = physical stress-tensor history; Eps = physical total-strain history; f−1/t−1 = fully reversed normal/torsional fatigue limits; f0/t0 = R=0 strengths, with amplitude-versus-maximum convention checked in the original; CA/VA = constant/variable amplitude; P/NP = proportional/non-proportional; F = fatigue strength/limit; L = life; Dmg = accumulated damage. “CA P/NP” is the proposed first implementation scope, not proof of validation for every possible NP path. A bibliographic source is still required before coding every new criterion.

**Specific equivalence check, not a new implementation:** the review's scalar Jiang form is FP = σmax Δε + Ω Δγ Δτ. In the repository, MSWT = a ⟨σmax⟩Δε/2 + (1−a)ΔγΔτ/4. For 0 < a ≤ 1, nonnegative normal maxima and identical range definitions, Ω = (1−a)/(2a) makes FP = (2/a) MSWT. This is a conditional algebraic equivalence only; clipping under compression, original parameter domains, native life laws and incremental history treatment can break equivalence. These overlaps should be checked before counting new methods.

## 10. Recommended order and shared dependencies

1. **Close existing source gaps without changing established behavior prematurely.** Retrieve the unresolved LIU2021/MKBM/MGSE_ZHU/LI originals and resolve Jiang/MSWT scope. Keep BP/CAIM and the corrected pipeline as the regression baseline.
2. **Add a compact but diverse first batch:** N01 Crossland, N02 Sines, N03 Papadopoulos, N04 MWCM, N05 Wang 2022, N06 Zhang–Yao, N07 Qi, N08 Gonçalves and N09 Matsubara. N01/N05/N06/N08 are the most straightforward code additions; N02/N04/N09 need particular calibration data. Include von Mises N38 as a transparent baseline.
3. **Restore mathematically defined classical methods:** R01 Carpinteri–Spagnoli and R02 Dang Van, then R03 deviatoric SWT. Their source corrections are more valuable than registering another incomplete proxy.
4. **Expand mean-shear and native-life comparisons:** N10, N11/N12, N15, N16 and N17. Add N13/N14 when a proportional baseline comparison is useful. Preserve material-domain guards and correct axial-versus-bending calibration.
5. **Build path geometry once, then reuse it:** S02 plus N18/N19/N20/N21/N22/N23/N24/N37/N53. N18/N23/N37 require real native-life support rather than scalar post-fit substitution. S01 can accelerate compatible searches, and S03 improves calibration after the equations are settled.
6. **Add strain/energy and specialist candidates when matching data arrive:** N25–N32, N34–N36, N42–N52 and N54–N57. Low priority is not rejection. Methods requiring numerous measured coefficients should wait for those coefficients or a clearly specified identification dataset.
7. **Treat C work as separate projects:** first a general time-history/cycle/damage layer; independently, a spatial FE/notch/defect layer. A constitutive solver, continuum-fatigue integrator, crack-growth workflow and composite RVE are additional distinct projects, not side effects of those two layers.

### Minimum reference package for each selected method

Supply the full original article, supplementary equations/errata and any author code; the exact material constants with units and test condition; the raw or tabulated loading histories and experimental lives/limits; and at least one worked prediction with the expected intermediate parameter and, where relevant, critical-plane orientation. For mean-stress models, include reversed and repeated axial/torsion cases. For NP models, include an ellipse and at least one path that distinguishes the claimed path measure. For strain models, include measured or independently computed stabilized total-strain histories. For native life models, provide their actual calibration curves, not only two endurance limits.

Subsequent validation should separate calibration and assessment data, recover the pure axial/torsion limits, check coordinate rotation and phase-origin invariance, test boundary/degenerate cases, and compare against original numerical examples. Preserve all prior tests and the all-slope log10(DP_e/DP) output. Probabilistic, life-native and accumulated-damage results need their own semantics instead of being forced into that ratio.
