# Usage of Böhme–Papuga and CAIM

To choose between the two criteria, set `MODEL_NAME = "BP"` or `MODEL_NAME = "CAIM"` in `scripts/1_run_material.py`, or pass the same selection with `multifat-run --model BP`. The required inputs in either case are `Sigm1` and `Taum1`, namely the tensile and torsional fatigue limits at R=−1. Where they exist, the columns `Tensile fatigue strength R=0` and `Torsional fatigue strength R=0` may be added to the workbook `Summary` sheet. Values of this kind are maximum stresses for zero-to-maximum loading. Should they be unavailable, the runner obtains them from tensile strength and the R=−1 limits by means of the estimates in Eqs. (1)–(2) of [Böhme, Papuga & Lange (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1111/ffe.70244), and reports the values so obtained. The BP damage parameter is dimensionless; it relies on a sphere integral of the shear and normal amplitudes and of the mean stresses. A more recent and separate hybrid formulation from the same paper is CAIM: a critical-plane maximum with different coefficients enters its amplitude term, while a sphere integral still constitutes its mean-stress term. The two are retained for comparison, and neither requires an `_ext` suffix. CAIM follows the equations of the supplied `damage_criteria_CAIM.m`, including the piecewise normal-amplitude coefficient and the clipping of negative squared damage to zero.

Single-harmonic stress histories are supported by both implementations, and the shear amplitude is computed as the major semi-axis of the shear ellipse. The sphere integration is carried out with 24 × 48 Gauss–Legendre/azimuth samples. Several sampled maxima are refined numerically by CAIM; an exact reproduction of the MATLAB angular grid is not achieved, nor is an exact global maximum guaranteed. Analytical loading limits, together with a comparison against an independent dense angular evaluation of the supplied equations, form the coverage of the focused tests.
